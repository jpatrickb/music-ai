## On the whole set

Okay, so I actually have a lot of feedback that relates to pretty much all of the pieces since I
noticed that they all pretty much make a lot of the same choices as others and I think they're not
really very great choices, but they're pretty common and so I think it's worth pointing out and
talking about them.

I think the most obvious one is the left hand patterns. It's sort of a bouncing bass. Um you know,
fifths, sixths, octaves, the left hand just bouncing up and down. And then in a lot of places, a
lot of the songs will use arpeggios as well.

Sometimes skipping notes, sometimes not. Um I think the left hand texture needs a lot more
variation. Um it's pretty bland having the exact same arpeggio patterns just repeated the entire
way through. There's uh you know next to no rhythmic variation in the left hand throughout so many
of the songs.

Um I was impressed with the first Noel, the first version of the first Noel that uh had some
quarter notes in the left hand instead of eighth notes. Um And then the second arrangement of Hark
the Herald Angels Sing had some quarter notes as well, which was cool.

The triplets in O Come All Ye Faithful were good and the sixteenth notes in Joy to the World were
good, but I think a lot of the arpeggios suffer some of the same patterns. Um and that is that You
know, a lot of times the seventh chords, you know, typically the seventh will be higher in pitch.

You know, for example in a dominant seventh um and when the left hand does these arpeggios it'll
often have the seventh lower down, and since the seventh is a very dissonant note. Having it in the
lower registers makes the dissonance sound really muddy and instead of having a good dissonance
with tension and release, it just sounds really muddy and unclear.

And so that's one mistake that is made in general that needs to be improved. Um in some places the
jumps are just too big. Um you know, sometimes there will be jumps in an arpeggio that is greater
than an octave, which is typically not recommended.

And then other times they'll have a jump of an octave followed by another large jump such as a
fourth or a fifth. And that's just too big of a stretch for your hand and having fingers crossing
because when you have a jump that's an octave or more or well near an octave, you're going to have
to have your fingers switch over each other.

And so then when you have a large jump followed by another large jump, you have to cross
immediately into a large jump, but your fingers can't hit a large jump that requires crossing at
the same time. And so larger jumps or sections that cover a large range on the piano, they need to
have some of the higher notes be closer together.

Um that being said, as we're in the very low registers, you want to avoid notes being close
together as much as possible. Um sometimes a fifth is the closest that you want them to be,
otherwise it gets too muddy. Um anyways, that's some advice for the arpeggios.

I don't necessarily love the rhythmic pattern of just the constant eighth note arpeggios and jumps
and I think that needs to be changed. I think one of the issues that we had in this first round of
hymns for Christmas is that the instructions um didn't have really any instructions on style.

It's pretty much just uh the form of the piece. Um and so I think that it would be better to try to

Um these are all important considerations in arranging. Um so that being said, I'll move on to some

## On the modulations

One other thing that I will add, the modulations in most cases were really quite terrible. It
seemed like in most cases the modulation strategy was to just suddenly hit the dominant chord of
the new key and then land in the new key. And this was pretty terrible.

There was practically no modulation, there was no preparation, there was no moving around in an
interlude or in between space, and most of the modulations took place across one to two, maybe in
some cases three or four measures, which is much too quick, I think.

Maybe I mean for some modulation styles, like a common tone modulation, there's not necessarily a
preparation that's required because it's a modulation that's not super jolting. But that strategy
was never employed, and the strategies that were employed did not really work.

And I don't know, I mean maybe they started on the right track, but the execution was just not
there. And in cases where we have tricky modulations or you know, where we use something like an
augmented six modulation, the voicing really matters a lot, and the use of non-chord tones to help
increase the tension in a satisfying way is really useful, and most of the modulations in this case
were done quite poorly.
