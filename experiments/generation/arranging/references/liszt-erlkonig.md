# Liszt: Erlkönig (Schubert D.328), S.558/4

Sources: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-erlkonig-s558-4.krn` and `data/lieder/kern/schubert-erlkonig.krn`.
Both have 148 bars, and they line up **bar for bar**, so one bar number serves both. C4 = middle C.

## 1. Overview

Liszt keeps Schubert's form, keys, harmony and running bass almost unchanged. His work is
redistribution. One pianist now plays the voice and both piano hands, so each character's tune goes
in its own register, hand and texture. The **narrator** sits in the middle, inside the repeated
chords. The **father** is always in the left hand, an octave below the voice, under the texture. The
**son** is high, either as short detached left-hand chords crossed over the right hand, or as an
E-flat-against-D grinding octave inside sweeping broken octaves marked *tremante*. The **Erlking**
gets a new texture each time: the tune in high octaves over a lighter, dance-like accompaniment
that Liszt reworks from Schubert's.

## 3. Sections

**Intro (1–14).** Schubert's RH triplet octaves stay as they are. His single-note LH run (G2–E♭3,
bar 2) is doubled into octaves an octave lower (G1+G2 up to E♭2+E♭3).

**Narrator (15–31).** At 15–18 the LH plays the bass run, then jumps to the tune in octaves
(A3+A4, B♭3+B♭4). The RH dyads (D4+G4) repeat *inside* that octave, so the hands interlock. At 20
the LH takes over the repeated chords (A3+D4+F♯4) so that the RH is free. At 21–28 the RH holds the
tune note on top (D5 for three beats) while the same hand keeps repeating dyads below (F4+G4).
Liszt divides Schubert's four-note RH chords between the hands. At 31 (*molto energico*, f) he fills Schubert's single LH notes into chords (C2+G2+A2+E♭3).

**Father (36–40, 51–56, 80–85, 105–112).** The father is always low and in the left hand: an
octave below Schubert's voice (37: G3–D3 against Schubert's G4–D4; 81: B3; 106–110: C♯4 down to
E3). At 36–39 the LH plays only the tune, with no bass, under the RH octaves, *sotto voce ma
marcato*. At 51–55 Liszt thins Schubert's F3+F4 octaves to one repeated F in each hand (F4 and F3).
The tune (C3, E♭3, F3, D3) lies *below* the repeating F3, so the player has to bring out a low inner
voice under a pedal note. At 105–111 the tune is in bare LH octaves, doubled down to three octaves
(C♯2+C♯3+C♯4, D2+D3+D4).

**Son (41–50, 72–79, 97–104, 123–130).** At 41–49 the LH crosses above the RH's repeated chords
and plays short eighth-note chords (42: F4+A♭4+B4+D5, then B3+D4+G4), where Schubert's voice holds
D5 for three beats. The line becomes breathless (*ansioso*). In the cries "Mein Vater" (72–79)
Schubert has the RH repeat D4+D5 octaves under the voice's E♭5. Liszt turns that into single notes
sweeping D4–D5–D6–D7 and back in triplets (72, 74, 76), marked *tremante*. On the tune bars (73,
75) the RH plays the tune as E♭4+E♭5 octaves, alternating with a D4+E♭4 semitone dyad. The LH
strikes the same dyad, which keeps Schubert's clash between voice and piano inside one hand. At
77–79 the rising chromatic tune (B♭4–A4–B♭4–B4–C5–C♯5–D5) goes into the LH as the top of tenths over
Schubert's bass (G3+B♭4, …, B3+D5). It sounds between the RH's alternating D4 and D5. Each cry is a
step higher: D (72), E (97), F (123).

**Erlking (57–71, 86–96, 117–122).** Erlking 1 is an octave above Schubert's voice. It is played as
rolled four-note RH chords with the tune on top (58: F4+D5+F5+D6, marked as arpeggiated), ppp
*leggiero*, and it keeps the turn at 64 (B5–C6–D6–C6). Schubert's accompaniment has RH triplet
chords over LH duple octave eighths. Liszt merges this into one LH figure: bass, chord, fifth
(B♭2 / B♭3+D4 / F3). In Erlking 2 (87–96) Schubert's RH arpeggios move into the LH as a rocking
figure (C2+C3, G3, C4+E4, C4+E4, G3, C3). The RH plays the tune in staccato octaves (G4+G5) with
staccato dyads (C5+E5) between and inside them, *amorosamente*. Erlking 3 (117–119) keeps
Schubert's repeated-chord accompaniment but in four layers. The RH plays the tune in octaves
(E♭5+E♭6) and its inner fingers repeat dyads (E♭5+G5). The LH strikes E♭2+E♭3, the pedal holds it,
and the LH leaps up to repeat chords in the treble (G3+B♭3+E♭4+G4). Schubert marks pp at 117 and
Liszt writes *molto appassionato*. ff arrives at 121 ("Gewalt").

**Ending (131–148).** In the kern, bars 131, 133, 135, 137 and 139–143 are written as straight
eighths in parallel octaves in both hands (G1+G2 with G3+G4) instead of Schubert's triplets. The
even bars keep triplets. Check this against a printed score. The tune first appears as accented
held notes inside the octaves (133: D2 and D4, accented, in both hands). From 135 it is the top of
the RH chords. The recitative (146) is played in bare octaves in both hands after one chord.
Schubert's last two notes (C♯4–D4, "todt") go into the LH bass octaves (C♯2+C♯3, D2+D3) under the
final chords.

## 4. Devices worth reusing

- **One register per character** (36–40, 41–49, 57–71). Each speaker gets a fixed hand, register
  and texture, which makes a dialogue clear without words.
- **Tune in octaves enclosing a filler** (15–18, 87–96). The repeated harmony sits inside the
  octave of the tune, so it never covers the tune.
- **One hand holds the tune and repeats the harmony** (21–28, 117–122). The outer finger holds the
  long tune note while the inner fingers keep the pulse.
- **Pass the figuration between hands** (19–20, 41–49). Move the accompaniment to whichever hand
  is free, so the tune can sit in the best register.
- **Recompose the accompaniment per verse** (58 vs 87 vs 117). The harmony stays the same while the
  figure changes, so the rhythm marks each new stanza.
- **Keep a dissonance by putting both notes in one hand** (73, 98, 124). This keeps the
  voice-against-piano clash once the voice is gone.
- **Thin out for intimacy and thicken for crisis** (51–55 against 105–111).

## 5. Playing demands

- **Repeated triplet chords and octaves** in the RH through most of 1–56, 80–86 and 105–122. The
  encoding has no ossia, so it is unclear whether Liszt eases this in places.
- **Voicing** a tune buried in the middle or bottom of the texture: 15–18 (interlocked hands), 51–55
  (low inner voice under a repeated F), 77–79 (LH tenths between RH octaves), 133 (accented inner
  notes inside fff octaves). In 87–96 and 117–119 the outer fingers must sing over light inner
  dyads in the same hand.
- **Stretches and leaps.** LH tenths at 77–79 and 102–104 (up to C♯4+E5 at 104). Rolled RH chords
  spanning a 13th (F4–D6) at 58–71. LH leaps of two to three octaves within a beat at 41–49 and
  117–119. Three-octave broken-octave sweeps in triplets (72, 123).
- **Speed and stamina.** The end is fff *il più presto possibile* in parallel octaves in both hands.
