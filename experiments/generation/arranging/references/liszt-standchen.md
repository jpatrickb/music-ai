# Liszt, Ständchen (Schubert D.957/4), S.560/7: technique notes

Sources: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-standchen-s560-7.krn` (L) and `data/lieder/kern/schubert-standchen-d957.krn` (S).
Bars are counted by barlines from the start of each kern file (S has the strophe inside a repeat, bars 5–36,
with bar 37 as the second-time ending). Every claim below was checked against the parsed notes.

## 1. Overview

Liszt keeps Schubert's key (D minor → D major), harmony and almost every melody note. He plays the strophe
three times instead of twice, each time in a different place in the texture: at pitch on top (L5–28), an
octave lower in the tenor with the accompaniment wrapped around it (L38–61), and at pitch with a new
one-beat-delayed echo an octave above, a canon (L71–90). Schubert already has the piano echo the
voice's cadences. Liszt keeps those echoes and makes them a contrast of register, then adds his own canon.
The middle section and climax follow Schubert closely, adding a chromatic cadenza run at the high point (L106).

## 3. Section by section

**Strophe 1 (L5–28).** The tune is note-for-note Schubert's vocal line at pitch (L5–8 = S5–8), with the
same triplet turns and dotted rhythms. Liszt splits Schubert's RH off-beat dyads between the hands. The RH
plays the tune and, under it, a single-note off-beat line (L5: D4–F4–D4–F4–D4; L11: F4–A4 alternating, a
third higher than Schubert). The LH holds the bass octave for the full bar (Schubert: half note) and plays
the off-beat dyads an octave down (L5: F3A3 / A3D4). Schubert's grace-note turn into S19/S25 becomes two
written-out 32nds at the end of L18, L20, L24. The piano echoes at L9–10 and L15–16 are Schubert's
(S9–10, S15–16), in the same register: after the voice cadences on F4 (L8), the RH answers A5–G5–G F E–F5
an octave up. Liszt only adds A4 to the chord in L9 and fills the LH dyads into triads. In L21–22 he keeps
Schubert's doubling of the voice a third above (voice G4–F♯4–G4–B♭4, piano B♭4–A4–B♭4–D5), both in the RH.
The harmony is Schubert's throughout. The only change found is L21 beat 3, where the lower line takes E4
instead of the voice's G4.

**Strophe 2 (L38–61): tune in the tenor.** The upper staff switches to bass clef (L38) and carries the tune
alone, an octave below the voice (A3–B♭3–A3–D4), marked *espressivo il canto* with accents. The lower staff
plays the bass on the beat (single D2, not an octave) and then leaps above the tune: L38 is D2, F3, **F4**,
F3A3, **D4F4**, F3, so the LH's off-beat notes sit above the RH's D4. The tune is in the middle of the
texture with accompaniment on both sides. The echoes (L42–43, L48–49) stay where Schubert has them, in
treble clef at A4–A5, so each call in the tenor is answered about two octaves higher. That is the
call-and-response I noticed. It begins on the downbeat right after the tune's cadence note (the
tune's F3 in L41 is held one bar, then L42 answers). In L54 the voice line (G4–F♯4–G4–B♭4) and Schubert's
companion line, dropped an octave (B♭3–A3–B♭3–D4), now sound a sixth apart with the companion
*below*: Schubert's third, inverted. One clear harmonic change: on the last eighth of L47, Liszt adds
A♭3–B♮3 over the F bass with D5 on top, a diminished-seventh passing chord (B–D–F–A♭) into the C7 echo at L48.
Schubert's S14 has only F4/D5 there. Strophe 2 closes with the tune in octaves inside full chords (L60–61,
G4–E5–G5).

**Interlude 2 (L62–70).** Same as S29–36, but beats 2–3 of L62 and L66 become a nine-note arpeggio in
sixths that rises from G4/B♭4 to B♭5/D6 and falls back. Marked *dolciss.* at L66.

**Strophe 3 (L71–90): the canon echo.** The tune returns at pitch (A4–B♭4–A4–D5). A second voice an octave
higher enters exactly one beat later with the same turn and leap (L71: A5–B♭5–A5–D6 starting on beat 2),
then stops after the leap. The same happens in L72, L75–76 (up to F6, E6) and L79 (A5–C♯6–F6). For the
cadence phrase the echo comes later and in shorter notes: the tune's A4–G4–G F E–F4 (L73–74) is answered
A5…G5–G F E–F5 within L74, and likewise in L77–78 (C6…). In L83 and L87 the echo is harmonised in sixths
(L87: G5/B5–F♯5/A♯5–G5/B5–B5/D6). In L80 and L86 the tune's falling A4–F4 is written in the lower staff, so
the LH takes the tune for two notes while the RH finishes the echo (F6–E6–D6–A5–F5) above. That is the tune
passing between hands. The LH keeps Schubert's original off-beat dyads (A3D4/D4F4) over a single bass note.
L89 restates the E5–D♯5–E5 turn an octave higher inside the same bar, and L90 holds D5–F♯5–D6 (= S37,
the second-time ending). Liszt goes from there straight into the middle section without the interlude.

**Middle section (L91–102).** Schubert's dialogue (the voice sings the dotted A–C♯–E arpeggio, the piano
repeats it a bar later in octaves) is kept, but Liszt moves the answers down. In L92 the RH octaves sing
D–C♯ (the voice of S39) while the LH plays the dotted echo A2–C♯3–E3 in the bass. In L93 the D–C♯ echo
goes into inner voices (RH D4→C♯4, LH D3→C♯3) under the tune. In L94 the motif A–C♯–E–D is answered in the
middle (RH A3–C♯4–E4–D4, doubled by the LH an octave down). The F♯7 bar (L95) has grace-note chords in both
hands; L97–98 put the B–C♯–D–B figure in octaves on top of full chords (f).

**Climax and coda (L103–115).** The voice's D5 / B♭4 / A4 / F♯4 (S50–53) appear as the top notes D6,
B♭5, A5 of large chords (L103–105). Liszt adds D to Schubert's E–G–B♭ chord over G in L104. Quintuplet
arpeggios lead into L104 and L105. L106 is an unaccompanied chromatic run from G♯5 to E♯7 (22 notes, after
the A-major chord on beat 1) that lands on D7/F♯7 (L107), the voice's final F♯ three octaves up. The postlude is Schubert's
(L107–112 = S53–58). Liszt replaces the final chord with two bars of D-major arpeggios spanning D2–A6
(L113–114, ppp) and a closing 4/4 bar (L115).

## 4. Devices worth reusing

- **Change the tune's register on each verse.** Top (L5), tenor (L38), top with a canon (L71). The notes
  hardly change; the listener follows the tune into a new place in the texture.
- **Accompaniment on both sides of a middle-register tune.** The bass below, off-beat chords above
  (L38–41). The tune has to be voiced through the texture.
- **Echo the tune in a different register.** A tenor call answered two octaves higher (L41→42), or a
  treble call answered in the bass (L92) or the middle (L94).
- **Canon at the octave, one beat late, only for the head motif** (L71–72, L75–76, L79). A short echo
  is enough. For the cadence phrase, delay and shorten it (L73–74).
- **Swap a third for a sixth.** Move the companion line below the tune by dropping it an octave (L54 vs L21).
- **Hand the tune to the other hand for a few notes** while the first hand ornaments above (L80, L86).
- **Keep the harmony and recolour one passing chord** (the diminished seventh at L47) instead of
  reharmonising.
- **Save the virtuoso gesture for the climax.** One cadenza run on the top note of the song (L106).

## 5. Playing demands

- **Voicing.** L38–61 are the hardest to voice. The tenor tune is in the RH while LH chords sound above
  it (L38 F4 and D4F4 against the RH's D4), so the tune must be brought out through notes higher than
  itself. In L71–90 the RH plays the tune and the echo together; the encoding gives alternating dynamics
  (p/mf/pp/f in L71–78) but which voice gets which is unclear.
- **Leaps and crossings.** In L38–59 the LH jumps from the bass (D2) to above the RH tune (F4) within a
  beat, more than two octaves, so the hands cross or interlock every bar. L80/L86 put the tune in the LH
  staff at A4–F4, which needs another crossing or shared hands; the encoding doesn't say which hand
  plays what.
- **Stretches.** As written, the RH holds the tune's D5 while the echo plays F6 (L75 beat 3, a tenth) and
  E6 (L76, a ninth). Whether the pedal or the other hand is meant to cover this is unclear.
- **Bravura.** Arpeggios in nines (L62, L66), grace chords in both hands (L95), and the chromatic run
  (L106). Overall, medium-hard Liszt: the problems are voicing and hand coordination more than speed.
