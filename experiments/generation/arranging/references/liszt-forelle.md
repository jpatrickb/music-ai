# Liszt, *Die Forelle* (Schubert D.550), S.564: technique notes

Sources: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-forelle-s564.krn`, `data/lieder/kern/schubert-forelle.krn`. Liszt bar
numbers are the printed ones (each cadenza, written over several bars in the file, keeps one
number). Schubert bars are counted by barlines from the start of his file, which has no piano
intro (bar 1 = vocal pickup; 1–25 repeated strophe, 25–39 dramatic stanza, 40–48 closing lines).
Claims were checked against note dumps; readings of what a passage depicts are marked as
interpretation.

## 1. Overview

Liszt keeps Schubert's melody and harmony almost untouched and arranges by **moving the tune
around inside the texture**. He states the strophe three times instead of Schubert's two: in the
middle register split between the thumbs (bars 13–33), on top at the original pitch (38–57), and
in the left hand as the top of marcato chords under high right-hand glitter (61–80). Then come the
dramatic stanza (86–99) and the closing lines an octave up (100–107). Schubert's sextuplet
accompaniment figure is the common thread: kept at pitch, split between the hands, sequenced in
the links, dissolved into cadenzas. Liszt adds his own intro (1–12) and coda (108–115), and prints
the stanza 1 words under the notes (14–32).

## 3. Section notes

**Introduction (1–12).** The intro is built only from Schubert's accompaniment cell (sextuplet
upbeat A♭3–F4–D♭4–A♭4–F4 into an eighth D♭5). It is broken by rests and echoed an octave down by the
lower staff (bar 2). Bar 3 restates the cell on F♯–A–C♯: F♯ minor, i.e. G♭ minor,
the minor iv of D♭. Bars 5–7 are D major (E♭♭, the Neapolitan), with the lower staff crossing above
the right hand (D6/A5, F♯6/D6, bars 6–7). Bars 8–10 alternate A♭7 and D major-seventh every beat,
two roots a tritone apart, under *cresc., accel.* The cadenza (10–11, written in 4/4 and 6/4) falls
from E♭7 in a sequence of four-note descending cells on G♭–A♭–B♭–D♭–E♭ (pentatonic over the A♭
dominant), passes into the left hand, and touches A♮2 before landing on the *fz* A♭7 chord of bar 12.
(Interpretation: a darting fish.)

**Strophe 1 (13–33): three-hand texture.** Schubert's right-hand figure is kept **at its exact
pitch** (bar 14 = Schubert bar 2: A♭3 F4 D♭4 A♭4 F4 | D♭5 A♭4). The vocal tune sits an octave lower,
*inside* the figure: the note on each beat 1 is held by the RH thumb (D♭4, bar 14), and the beat-2
notes go to the LH's upper voice (F4 F4, bar 14; the E♭4–D♭4–C4–B♭3 run, bar 16). The LH also plays
a deep bass (D♭2, A♭1) and off-beat chords. Three layers on two hands; the figure even starts
*below* the tune note (A♭3 under the held D♭4). Changes to Schubert are small: a chromatic A♮4 added to the rising figure
(bar 22), and the figure's top reshaped in bar 20.

**Strophe 2 (38–57).** The tune moves to the top at Schubert's pitch. The RH plays it over its own
sextuplet on beat 1. The figure then **moves to the LH on beat 2**, in the tenor (bar 38: LH D♭2,
chord, then F4–A♭3–D♭4–F3–A♭3), so the accompaniment becomes a dialogue between the hands. The ossia
(50–56) writes that LH beat-2 figure two octaves higher (D♭6–G♭5–B♭5…), above the tune: a
hand-crossing option.

**Links (57–60, 80–84, 109–112).** Schubert's rising chromatic tail (D♭–F–G♭–G♮–A♭) becomes a
sequence that climbs an octave each half bar, alternating hands, from D♭4 to F7, fading (*pp*, *ppp*)
rather than building.

**Strophe 3 (60–80).** *f, marcato la melodia, brioso.* The tune is the top note of LH four-note
chords (D♭3–F3–A♭3–D♭4, bar 61), with the low bass struck between them. Schubert's figure is gone.
In bars 61–68 the RH plays sextuplets of single notes alternating with octaves or sixths, climbing
two octaves in each bar (A♭4 … D♭7/F6, A♭6). In bars 69–79 it plays a 32nd-note cell: low note,
dyad an octave up, upper neighbour (A♭5, A♭6/E♭6, B♭6, A♭6/E♭6), shifted to fit each chord
(G♭–B♭–C in 73 and 77, D♭–A♭–E♭ in 74 and 78). A shimmering pedal two octaves above the tune
(interpretation: light on water).

**Dramatic stanza (85–99).** The bar of near-silence (85) and *ppp* before it set off
"Doch endlich." Liszt keeps Schubert's harmony: F7/A to B♭ minor (86–89), then E♭7/G alternating
with B♭ø7/A♭ (90–92, "er macht das Bächlein tückisch trübe"). His intensifications:
- Schubert's repeated D♭5 (bars 31–33) is **displaced up by octaves**: D♭5, D♭6, then D♭7 (90–91),
  over RH arpeggios rising through three octaves and LH broken chords.
- Bar 92: *f*, a tremolo between D♭7/B♭6 and E♭6/D♭6 over a rising LH arpeggio.
- Bar 93: a *fz* E♭7 chord, then an **inserted cadenza** that Schubert doesn't have. It is a
  chromatic cascade of rising four-note cells (A6–B♭6–B6–C7, A♭6–A6–B♭6–B6, …), each a semitone
  lower than the last, falling from C7 to D5 under *diminuendo, ritard.* and resolving to E♭5 at
  bar 94. It sits between "trübe" and "eh ich es gedacht". (Interpretation: the muddied water.)
- Bars 94–99 (*capriccioso*) keep Schubert's repeated sixteenth-note chords for "so zuckte seine
  Rute … zappelt dran". One melodic change: a held E♭5 where Schubert has C5 (bar 95).

**Closing lines (100–107).** The tune goes up an octave (B♭5–E♭6) and is thickened with octaves and
chords. The sextuplet figure returns, now LH on beat 1 and RH on beat 2, the reverse of strophe 2.

**Cadenza and coda (108–115).** Bar 108 falls in two interleaved layers over the dominant: A♭7
(G♭–E♭–C–A♭) against A diminished (A–G♭–E♭–C). The LH ends on B♭♭3 (= A♮), the same chromatic
upper neighbour the intro cadenza touched. Bar 113 answer the opening cadenza with a falling
D♭-pentatonic cascade (F–E♭–D♭–B♭–A♭) over a held LH B♭5, marked *dolciss.* It ends on *ppp*
D♭ chords spread over four octaves (114–115).

## 4. Reusable devices (techniques, not rules)

- **Move the tune to a new register/position each verse** (middle 13–33, top 38–57, LH chord-top
  61–80); melody intact, texture new.
- **Keep the original accompaniment at pitch and slot the tune in under it** (14–33).
- **Pass one figure between the hands** (38–56, 100–107), and reverse the direction for a later
  verse.
- **Turn a cadence tail into a rising sequence across octaves** to link verses (57–60, 80–84),
  fading rather than building.
- **Displace repeated melody notes by octaves** for climax (90–91).
- **Insert a short written-out cadenza at the dramatic peak** (93) and over the final dominant
  (108), and end on a cadenza that mirrors the opening one (10–11 vs 113).
- **Use silence before the turn in the story** (85).
- **Colour an introduction with a remote key and alternate it with V7** (F♯m/D vs A♭7, bars 3–10)
  before the first verse in plain diatonic harmony.

## 5. Playing demands

- **Voicing.** Strophe 1 (the three-hand passage, 14–33): tune inside the RH figure, passed
  between thumbs. Strophe 2: RH 5th finger over its own sextuplets. Strophe 3: LH chord tops
  projected under a loud, fast RH.
- **Leaps.** The LH goes from a deep bass to the tenor figure in every bar of strophe 2 (D♭2 to F4,
  bar 38). The ossia (50–56) crosses the LH above the RH. In 61–68 the RH climbs two octaves per
  bar, and the lower staff crosses above the RH in bars 6–7. In strophe 3 the LH strikes a low bass
  between held chords (bars 62, 70); how Liszt expects that to be sustained (pedal, rolling) is
  unclear from the notes.
- **Speed and endurance.** Sextuplet sixteenths throughout; 32nd-note rotations (69–79);
  20- and 17-note groups per half bar in the cascade (93); dense runs in the cadenzas (10–11,
  108, 113).
- **Difficulty.** Advanced; hardest in strophe 3 and the dramatic stanza (a judgment, not a grade).
