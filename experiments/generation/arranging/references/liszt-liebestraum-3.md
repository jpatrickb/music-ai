# Liszt, Liebestraum No. 3, S.541: technique notes

Sources: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-liebestraum-3-s541.krn` and `data/liszt/kern/liszt-o-lieb-song-s298.krn`
(S, Liszt's own song). Bar numbers are the printed ones; the opening pickup is bar 0, and each
cadenza, though written over several bars in the file, keeps one number (bars 25 and 59). Song bars
(S) are printed and counted alike. Every claim below was checked against the parsed notes.

## 1. Overview

The piece is the song with the voice moved into the piano. Liszt keeps the song's tune, keys, and
harmony almost bar for bar for three verses: A♭, then B major (moving through C, A minor, E), then A♭.
What changes is where the tune sits. Verse 1 puts it in the middle, an octave below the voice, passed back
and forth between the thumbs. Verse 2 puts it on top over an arpeggio doubled in both hands, then in
octaves and chords. Verse 3 puts it back at the voice's pitch, with the left hand crossing over to play
chords above it. He replaces the song's two transitions with cadenzas, and the second cadenza also
replaces the song's C-major recitative (S56–76), which is cut. Before that he adds a climax of his own
(bars 49–58).

## 3. Sections

**Verse 1 (bars 0–24).** The tune matches S3–21 note for note, an octave lower. The texture is a bass note
on beats 1 and 4, then a RH arpeggio in off-beat eighths above (bar 1: E♭4–A♭4–C5–A♭4–E♭4), as in the song's
accompaniment. The tune sits under the arpeggio, and the hand that plays it changes, sometimes on every note:

- Bar 1: RH C4 (beat 1), LH C4 (beat 4).
- Bar 3: RH C4, LH C4, RH D♭4, LH C4: four notes, four hand changes.
- Bar 5, bar 11: the LH plays both the bass E♭2 and the tune F3–G3–A♭3–C4–B♭3.
- Bar 10: LH F4, above the bottom of the RH arpeggio (D4).
- Bar 13: LH starts the A♭3, and the RH takes over at the G3 (beat 3½).
- bars 16–17: RH E4 on beat 1, LH E4 on beat 4.

The arpeggio never moves below the tune. The only thing below it is the LH bass, so the arpeggio is
above and the bass is below; there is no arpeggio underneath. bars 16–19 (S18–21) is the song's own
common-tone move: the tune holds E4 while the chords go C → a → E, then C♯ minor. At bar 20 a D♯-major chord,
spelled sharp, is E♭ (V of A♭) heard enharmonically, and the next half bar is spelled flat. bars 20–22 squeeze
S22–25 into three bars by dropping one of the song's piano echoes of B♭–D♭–C♭. bar 23 is the descending
F♭4–A♭3 line alone in the LH. bar 24: G3, then an E♭7 arpeggio.

**Cadenza 1 (bar 25).** Its material is the song's F♭–E♭ sigh (S26) and the rising G–D♭–F♭–E♭ figure of
the song's transition bar (S28). It alternates D♭-minor-over-F♭ (iv6) with E♭ major (V), split as LH bass
note plus RH dyad, and climbs about three octaves to F♭7. It comes down in faster notes and stalls on a
trill-like alternation (RH A♭/D♭ against G/B♭, LH F♭ against E♭), then stops on a single D♮5. The next bar
starts on D♯5. Its function is to stretch out the dominant. The modulation is the song's: the E♭ of the
dominant becomes D♯, the third of B major. That is a chromatic-mediant common-tone modulation across an
enharmonic seam (theory/06, "Enharmonic Equivalence"). The common tone moves from degree 5 in A♭ to
degree 3 in B. Liszt adds D♮ as a lower neighbour into it.

**Verse 2 (bars 26–48).** Liszt follows the song's piano right hand, which carries the tune in octaves here
(S29–51). He drops the upper octave and puts a middle layer under the tune: both hands play the same
arpeggio in parallel sixths (bar 26: RH D♯4–F♯4–B4, LH F♯3–B3–D♯4), while the LH also takes the bass on
beat 1. The move to C is the song's, over a chromatic bass B–A♯–A–A♭–G (bars 32–34). At bar 33 the bass A2 is
under B♯°7, and the bass A♭2 is under A♭–C–E♭–F♯, a German sixth in C. That resolves to a cadential 6/4
(bar 34), then V9 and V7 (bars 35–36), then I (bar 37): theory/07, treatment 1. The tune holds D♯5 through both
chords, so it is D♯ in the first and E♭ in the second. From bar 38 the tune is on top of 4-note RH chords;
bar 40 goes to A minor and bar 41 to E major (octaves G♯5/G♯6). The return to A♭ is again an enharmonic common
tone. G♯5/G♯6 is held for all of bar 48 over a bass falling D–D♭, and in bar 49 it is respelled A♭. This is
theory/06's own example (A♭ ↔ E, A♭ = G♯), reversed. The song goes the other way here, from S52 toward C
major. At bar 49 beat 4 Liszt leaves it: the bass falls C–B–B♭ to B♭7 (bar 50), V of V.

**Climax (bars 49–58).** The head is the song's A♭–G–F (S52–55). After that it is Liszt's: a 2-bar unit
(bars 51–52) repeated over an E♭ pedal, RH octaves with a triplet turn (bar 52), and a run (bar 54) up to C♭7/G7.
bar 55 is a descending octave scale for the RH alone. bar 58 holds F–G–B♭–D♭–F (an E♭9 chord without its root)
while the LH climbs an E♭7 arpeggio from E♭2 to D♭6.

**Cadenza 2 (bar 59).** All of it stays on E♭ and comes down in four stages:

1. Interlocked chromatic major thirds, the hands alternating in 32nds, from E7/C7 down to about A4 (bar 59).
2. An E♭7 arpeggio down to D4 and back up to F7 (bar 59).
3. A descent in which D♭, B♭, G and F are each approached from a semitone below (C–D♭, A–B♭, F♯–G, E–F),
   marked *leggiero* (bar 59).
4. E♭4 alternating with a rising chromatic line D–B, then the eighth-note E♭4 that is the tune's pickup
   (bar 59).

The cadenza takes the climax back down from the top of the keyboard to the register where the tune
starts, and ends on its first note. Cadenza 1 does the same thing with D♮–D♯.

**Verse 3 (bars 60–75).** The tune is S78–93 at the voice's pitch, bar for bar, as the RH top note, with the RH
arpeggio under it in off-beat eighths. The LH plays the bass, then crosses above to take the song's
beat-3–4 chords (bar 60: E♭5–A♭5–C6). In bar 64 it plays the tune's last three notes an octave higher
(A♭5–C6–B♭5). The descending chromatic bass D–D♭–C–C♭–B♭ under diminished and half-diminished sevenths
(bars 69–73) is the song's (S87–91).

**Close (bars 80–84).** A major over C♯ is the Neapolitan sixth of A♭ (B𝄫–D♭–F♭ spelled sharp; theory/07,
"The Neapolitan Chord"). Then come E♭ (bar 81), an A♭ pedal with lower chromatic neighbours in the inner
voices (bar 82), and inner voices falling in parallel sixths over the pedal (bar 83).

## 4. Devices worth reusing

- **Tune in the thumbs, passed between the hands** (bars 1–14): a middle-register tune inside one
  accompaniment figure. Hand it to whichever thumb is free; the hand changes can fall on any note.
- **The same tune in three places**: middle 8vb, top, top inside the texture (bar 1, bar 26, bar 60). The verse
  changes and the tune doesn't.
- **An arpeggio doubled in sixths between the hands under a held top line** (bars 26–36): a fuller middle
  layer for a climactic verse.
- **The LH crossing over to supply the after-beat chords** (bars 60–68): it frees the RH for tune plus
  arpeggio.
- **An enharmonic common-tone hinge on a held tune note**: E♭ = D♯ (bars 25–26), G♯ = A♭ (bars 48–49). Hold the
  pitch, change the chord under it, and respell it.
- **A Ger+6 to cadential 6/4 to bring in a new key** (bars 33–37).
- **A cadenza built from the song's own motive that ends on the next verse's pickup note** (bar 25,
  bar 59).
- **A Neapolitan sixth just before the final V–I** (bars 80–81).

## 5. Playing demands

- **Voicing.** In verse 1 the tune is in the thumbs with arpeggio and bass on either side. Each thumb must
  sound over the eighths with nothing to bring it out, and the hand changes must not break the line
  (bar 3, bar 9, bar 13).
- **Stretches.** The LH has 10ths throughout bars 7–22 and bars 66–73 (e.g. A♭2+C4, F♭2+A♭3). There are 12ths at
  bar 10 (B♭2+F4), bar 16 (C2+G3) and bar 80 (C♯2+A3). These must be rolled, or the upper note taken by the free
  RH; the notation doesn't say which. The bass notes are written as quarters, so the harmony under the
  arpeggio depends on the pedal (*sempre Pedale* from bar 6).
- **Octaves held by pedal.** In bars 41–48 the RH strikes G♯5/G♯6 and then drops to an arpeggio between G♯4 and G♯5,
  so the octave lasts only through the pedal.
- **Hand crossing and leaps.** The LH goes from bass to chords two octaves up every bar in bars 60–68. In
  bars 26–48 it goes from bass to arpeggio.
- **Cadenzas.** Fast interlocked hands (bar 25, bar 59), a 48th-note tremolo (bar 25), and a 4-octave arpeggio
  (bar 58).
- **Difficulty.** Advanced: verse 1 is about balance; bars 38–58 need octave and chord stamina at ff.
