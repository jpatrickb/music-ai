# Liszt, "Un sospiro", Trois études de concert S.144 No. 3: technique notes

Source: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-un-sospiro-s144-3.krn`. Bar numbers are the printed ones; cadenza bars
keep one number (bar 38 spans the file's `=38` and `=38X1`). Every claim was checked against the
parsed notes.

Hands: the edition's footnote (attached to *cantando*, bar 3) says stems down are LH and stems up
are RH. That governs the top-staff tune in bars 3–20, 39–42 and 69–72. Elsewhere stems only
separate voices, so hands are read from the staff and marked *inferred*.

## 1. Overview

This is the model for one texture: a tune on the beat over an arpeggio that never stops. The two
hands share the tune by crossing, and they split the arpeggio between them. One rule decides every
note: the hand that is free takes the tune.

The theme is 5-6-1-2-3-2-6-1 in D♭ (A♭ B♭ D♭ | E♭ F E♭ B♭ | D♭, bars 3–5). It comes back in several
forms:

- single notes (bar 3);
- broken octaves (bar 13);
- the head alone, rising in A (bars 22–26);
- LH octaves in F (bar 30);
- chords (bar 55);
- an octave lower, inside the texture (bar 60).

The keys fall by major thirds: D♭ → A → F → C♯ minor (= D♭ minor) → D♭. The closing chords,
D♭ – B𝄫 (= A) – F – D♭ (bars 80–84), restate that cycle.

## 3. Sections

**Theme (bars 1–12).** Each half bar has 14 notes:

- The LH plays D♭2–A♭2–D♭3–F3 up and F3–D♭3–A♭2 down.
- The RH plays A♭3–D♭4–F4, the peak A♭4 on beat 2, and the same notes down.

From bar 3 a tune note falls on every beat:

- **Beats 2 and 4** are the arpeggio's peak. The LH is free between its rising and falling notes,
  so it crosses over to play the tune, and the arpeggio's own A♭4 is dropped there (bar 3 has 12
  middle-staff notes, where bar 1 has 14).
- **Beats 1 and 3.** The LH is at the bottom, so the RH plays the tune, then drops back into the
  arpeggio four sevenths of a beat later.

Over bars 3–12 the hand changes 27 times in 30 notes. The only breaks are at bars 11–12: the 16th
A♭4 then F5, and E♭5 then D♭5, both pairs in the RH. In bars 3–8 the tune sits just above the
arpeggio's top (F4/G♭4). Each tune note is an eighth, staccato under a slur, held by *sempre Pedale*, and marked
*cantando* and *dolce con grazia*. Phrase b runs F7 (bar 9), then B♭ minor, then D♭/A♭ to A♭7 (bar
11), and closes on D♭ (bar 12). The file prints *f* at bar 3 and *ff* at bar 19. Both clash with
*dolce* and may be encoding errors; unclear.

**Broken octaves (bars 13–18).** These are bars 3–8 again. Each tune note becomes an octave broken
by one hand: the lower note on the beat, the upper one a triplet-eighth later. Through bar 20 the
alternation is strict, 22 changes in 23 notes. Bar 18's second half turns to D♭ minor (F♭).

**To A (bars 18–22).** Bar 18's D♭ minor is enharmonically C♯ minor, which is iii of A. E7 follows
(bar 19), then A (bar 20). On beat 1 of bar 20 the tune's C♯5 is the old tonic D♭, now heard as the
new tonic's third. That is theory/06 Case 1, a major third down, smoothed by a borrowed tonic minor
(theory/04) that works like a pivot (theory/05).

Bars 19–20 put phrase b's shape into A (F♯5–E5–B4 against bar 9's G♭5–F5–C5). At bar 21 (ff
*appassionato*) the texture opens out:

- the LH plays the whole arpeggio alone, in sextuplets from D2 to F♯4;
- the RH plays the tune alone, in octaves, over B minor/D and E7.

Bar 22 cadences B → A, *dolce*.

**Head in A (bars 22–26).** The head 5-6-1-2-3 comes twice:

- **Bars 22–24.** First pp in the tenor: E3 F♯3 A3 B3 C♯4, then E4. These notes are in the RH staff,
  on the LH's beat rests, and the LH arpeggio climbs past them to C♯5.
- **Bars 24–26.** Then at the top, *crescendo*: E5 | F♯5 A5 B5 C♯6 | E6. E5, A5 and C♯6 are written
  in the bass staff, so the LH crosses over (inferred).

**To F (bars 26–30).** E6 is held over A, then repeated in octaves while the harmony moves to
E–G–B♭–D♭ and then C7 (bar 27). The E goes from fifth of A to third of F's V7. That is a theory/06
variant: the common tone lands on the new dominant, not the new tonic. A falling octave line over
F-minor harmony (bars 28–29) reaches F minor 6/4, then C7, then F major at bar 30 (mixture,
theory/04).

**Head in F (bars 30–34).** This is the dynamic peak (ff *impetuoso*, *marcato*). The head
C | D F G A | C is in LH octaves. A quick LH chord falls on the 16th before beats 2 and 4. The RH
plays sextuplet arpeggios with dyads up to A6. Only bar 30's first C4/C5 is in the RH staff, so the
LH carries the tune in the bass.

**Bars 35–38.** A chain of diminished sevenths and triads (D♯°7, A/C♯, G♯, F♯, F𝄪°7) moves toward
G♯; the exact functional reading is unclear. The cadenza (bar 38, *Presto*) falls in chromatic major
sixths from F♯7/A6 to about F♯3/D♯4. The first part is on one staff, so whether one hand or two plays
it is unclear. It ends slowing onto G♯.

**Thirds episodes (bars 39–42, 69–72).** A sigh opens it: A4 → G♯4 over G♯ (bar 39). Then dyads
alternate hands by stems. Bar 40: C♯5/A4 (R), E5/C♯5 (L), F♯5/D5 (R), A5/F♯5 (L). The chords
underneath are A/G♯, then D/F♯ (the Neapolitan of C♯ minor, theory/07), then V. Bars 69–72 bring
this into D♭: D-major and G-major dyads over a D♭ bass (spelled C♯3 in bar 72). D major is D♭'s
Neapolitan, spelled sharp.

**Bars 43–54.**

- **Bar 44.** F–A–C–D♯, a German sixth, resolves to A minor 6/4 (theory/07, treatment 1). The bass
  keeps falling to G♯1 (bar 47).
- **Bars 47–52** have no tune. The hands overlap between B♯3 and B♯5: the RH alternates A and D♯ in
  sextuplets, and the LH plays an accented B♯5–F♯5–B♯4–F♯4 in quintuplets. One diminished seventh,
  B♯–D♯–F♯–A, sits under G♯ (bars 47–49) and then under B (bars 50–52). G♯7♭9 is the home dominant
  A♭7♭9 spelled in sharps.
- **Bar 53** respells it as A♭7, with A falling to A♭. The *velocissimo* scales then fall about 3½
  octaves and climb back, in parallel sixths split between the hands.

**Bars 55–59** carry the heading "Variation for the extension of the cadenza for Auguste Rennebaum
in 1875". This is a later, alternative cadenza extension Liszt wrote for a student. It isn't in the
standard editions (the owner, who plays the piece, hadn't seen it), so treat it as optional, not as part
of the usual text. Phrase a appears as p chords, then a line falls from D♭5 to C♭4 and into bar 60.

**Return (bars 60–68).** Marked *un poco più mosso*, *dolce*. The tune comes back an octave below
bar 3 (A♭3 B♭3 D♭4 | E♭4 F4 E♭4 B♭3 | D♭4), accented, and each beat's figure starts on it:

- **Beats 2 and 4.** The RH plays the tune note, then sweeps through its octaves (bar 60, beat 2:
  A♭3 D♭4 A♭4 A♭4 D♭5 A♭5).
- **Beats 1 and 3.** The tune note heads the LH's triplet (B♭3–F3–D♭3).

So the tune sits where the hands meet and sounds in up to three octaves (hands inferred from the
staves; here the stems contradict the footnote). Bars 66–68 restate phrase b. Bar 68 is a 12/4
bar that sweeps up to A♭7.

**Coda (bars 73–85).**

- **Bars 73–76.** Triads fall by minor thirds (D♭, B♭, G, E), each with a seventh from a bass that
  steps down. The top line rises F6–F6–G6–G♯6, and G♯ = A♭ carries into D♭ (bar 77).
- **Bar 77.** Each spread chord has four groups, alternating hands upward: LH D♭2–F3, RH A♭3–F4,
  LH A♭4–F5, RH A♭5–A♭6.
- **Bar 78.** F–E♭–B♭ in octaves echoes bar 4. The D♭ it expects never comes; bar 79 is a lone
  E♭3, pp.
- **Bars 80–85.** D♭, B𝄫 and F chords over a bass falling from D♭3 to D♭1. The piece ends F → D♭.

## 4. Devices worth reusing

- **Give the tune to the free hand** (bars 3–20). The RH takes it while the LH is at the bottom of
  the arpeggio; the LH crosses over at the peak. The tune note *replaces* the arpeggio note at that
  onset.
- **One tune, many settings.** Single notes, broken octaves, bass octaves, middle voice with sweeps
  (bars 3, 13, 30, 60). The verse changes; the tune doesn't.
- **The head as development material**, low then high, in new keys (bars 22–26, 30–34).
- **Keys a major third apart, entered on a common tone** (bar 20; E held across bars 26–27;
  theory/06, theory/04).
- **One diminished seventh as an extended dominant**, respelled to resolve (bars 47–53).
- **A Neapolitan episode** in alternating dyads (bars 40, 70).
- **Triads falling by minor thirds back to the tonic** (bars 73–77), then a coda that sums up the
  key plan (bars 80–85).
- **Harp spreads alternating hands upward** (bars 77–78).

## 5. Playing demands

- **Crossing leaps.** In bars 3–12 the LH leaps from its arpeggio up to the tune and back in about
  one beat: median 19 semitones, largest 25 (bar 9, F3 → G♭5). In bars 13–20, measured to the top
  of the broken octave: median 27, largest 38 (bar 19, E3 → F♯6).
- **Voicing.** An eighth-note tune held by pedal over 7 notes per beat. The arpeggio is p
  *legatissimo*, and the handovers within each beat must not bump.
- **Overlapping hands** at bars 22–24 and 47–52, with five against six at bar 47.
- **Speed.** Chromatic sixths (bar 38), scales in sixths (bars 53–54), LH octaves at ff (bars
  30–34).
- **Difficulty.** Advanced. The work is evenness, the crossing, and keeping one line singing as it
  passes between the hands.
