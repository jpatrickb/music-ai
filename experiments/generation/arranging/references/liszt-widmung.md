# Liszt, Liebeslied (Widmung) (Schumann Op. 25/1), S.566: technique notes

Sources: `data/liszt/kern/liszt-widmung-s566.krn` (L) and `data/lieder/kern/schumann-widmung.krn` (S).
L bars are the printed numbers. S bars are counted by barlines (S1 is the piano intro, the voice
enters at S2). Claims were checked against the parsed notes. In L44–56 staves 1–2 are a simpler
version marked *Ossia*; these notes describe the main text (staves 3–4) unless they say otherwise.

## 1. Overview

Liszt keeps Schumann's keys (A♭, E major, A♭), harmony and almost every melody note. His changes
are about form and layout. He plays stanza 1 twice: on top at pitch (L4–15), then mostly in the LH
tenor under a new RH figure in sixths (L16–27). He adds a link and a silence before the E major section
(L27–31). The return gets a new texture with the tune inside the figuration (L44–57). The closing stanza
is repeated as an *fff* climax over the middle section's triplet chords (L58–67). The middle section
and the Ave Maria postlude stay close to Schumann.

## 3. Section by section

**Intro and stanza 1 (L1–15).** L1 is Schumann's left hand alone. L2–3 are S1 at pitch, played twice.
From L4 the right hand plays the tune on top and Schumann's RH figure under it (L4). Where the RH holds a chord with the tune, the figure moves to the LH and is doubled
in sixths and tenths (L8, L10). Schumann's grace-note C5 before B♭4 (S12)
is written out as an eighth (L14). Harmony unchanged.

**Stanza 1 repeated (L16–27): the tune in the middle.** Liszt's addition. The tune drops an octave and becomes the top note of LH chords. L16 opens with a chord that
spans a tenth (A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–C4), then continues C4 C4 E♭4 A♭3 A♭3 B♭3 (S2 an octave down). Above it the
RH plays a new figure in sixths, an octave higher than Schumann's (L16: E♭4/C5, A♭4/E♭5, C5/A♭5…), reaching F6 (L18) and B♭6 (L24). The figure's lower note sits just above the tune: E♭4 over
C4 (L16), A♭4 over G♭4 (L22). The tune changes staff twice. In L18 Schumann's E♭5–D♭5–C5 is in the RH at
pitch, with the LH taking Schumann's tenor line G4–F4–E♭4 a sixth below. The last note of L25 (D♭5) is
also in the RH. Everywhere else in L16–27 it is in the LH. The map's staff-3 hit at L21–23 comes from
chord tones in the RH sixths; note by note the tune is in the LH (L22: D♭4, D♭4, G♭4, G♭4). This is
my "three-hand" passage: bass, tune and figure are three layers on two hands, with the LH
playing both bass and tune and the RH figure wrapped tightly above.

**Link (L27–31).** Liszt's own, *a tempo, leggiero*, then *dim.* The sixths figure breaks into
fragments that alternate between the staves every half bar. Each bar is lower than the last: top A♭6
(L27), C6 (L28), E♭5 (L29), C5 (L30). L31 is an A♭1/A♭2 bass, then one rolled A♭ chord from A♭3 to
A♭6 on the second half-note beat, then silence to the end of the bar.

**Modulation and middle section (L32–43).** Liszt does not change Schumann's modulation. There is no
pivot chord, and the harmony goes straight from A♭ major to E major. He changes the approach. Schumann
goes from the voice's held A♭4 (S13) straight into E major (S14); Liszt puts four bars of thinning
texture and a bar of near-silence in between. The common tone survives at
the same pitch: the rolled chord in L31 contains A♭4, and L32's tune note is G♯4 (the voice's pitch),
held on top of the first E major chord. The middle section is Schumann's at pitch (triplet chords, LH
octaves, *p*), with Rückert's words printed from "Du bist die Ruh" (L32). Liszt
revoices the chords so they sit under the tune. Schumann's B3/E4/G♯4 becomes G♯3/B3/E4 under the held
G♯4 (L32), and A3/A4/E4 becomes A3/E4 under A4 (L33). Other changes are small:
- L36: Schumann's inner chromatic line E–F♯–E♯ moves to the LH as F♯3, E♯3, over the bass D2, C♯2.
- L38: the turn becomes a quintuplet, F♯4 G♯4 F♯4 E♯4 F♯4. The LH crosses up to take the triplet
  chords (C♯4/E4) while the RH plays it.
- L41–43: the bass goes down an octave (B1, A1), with *più cresc.*, *rinforz.* and *rit.*

**The return (L43–48).** As in Schumann, the tune repeats C♯5 four times over A major (L43). The next
downbeat is D♭5, the same pitch, over E♭7 (L44). Roots a tritone apart, joined by C♯ = D♭; Liszt keeps
that harmony too. The return is marked by colour instead:
*riten. ad lib., dolce armonioso, una corda, pp*. The texture is also new. The RH holds the tune on top
(D♭5, E♭5, C5) and plays a 16th-note arpeggio under it in the same hand (L44: D♭4–E♭4–G4–B♭4 and back).
The LH strikes E♭2 on the downbeat and then a chord *above* the tune on the second half-note beat (L44: E♭5–G5–B♭5–D♭6;
L45: up to C6). Figuration below, chords above. After rising arpeggios in both hands (L47), L48 is an inserted bar:
an E♭7 arpeggio falling in both hands from E♭7 to B♭2.

**Final stanza (L49–57).** The tune does not climb to the top staff in the main text. The map's
staff-1 hits at L50–54 are the ossia, which copies the stanza 1 texture (L49–53 of the ossia equal
L4–8). In the main text the RH plays 16th arpeggios from C4 to C7 and back in every bar. The tune
moves between layers:
- L49: the first C5 is in the RH; the rest (C4 C4 E♭4 A♭3 A♭3 B♭3) is in the LH tenor, the top of LH chords.
- L51: F5 at pitch in RH octaves. Then E♭4–D♭4–C4 in the LH, while each RH triplet group starts on the
  same note in a different octave (E♭7, D♭6, C5).
- L52–55: in RH octaves at pitch on the beats (A♭4/A♭5, D♭4/D♭5, B♭4/B♭5), with the arpeggio passed
  from the LH (L53, second quarter: C4–E♭5) to the RH (third quarter: A♭5–A♭6).
- L54, L56: single tune notes jump two octaves (C6/C7, D♭7) and return within the bar.

**Climax (L58–67).** Schumann's voice ends on A♭4 ("Ich!", S39). Liszt puts that word on the downbeat
of L58 and starts the stanza again in the same chord, *fff*. The tune is an octave higher (C6, F6 in
L60) as the top of four-note RH chords. The LH plays a low octave on the downbeat (A♭1/A♭2) and then
triplet chords (A♭3/C4/E♭4 in L58), the repeated-chord texture of the E major section. In L65–66
(*con somma passione*) the LH alternates bass notes (some as grace notes) with chords, and the RH tune
chords fall an eighth after the beat (L65: F6, D♭6, B♭5; L66: A♭5). The stanza lands on a held A♭ chord, *fp* (L67).

**Postlude (L67–73).** Liszt keeps Schumann's quotation of Schubert's *Ave Maria* at pitch: A♭4–G4–A♭4–C5–B♭4
over the falling bass F2–F♭2–E♭2 (L68 = S40, again L70 = S42), with the same inner C♭4–D4. He marks
it *expressivo*. Schumann ends with two chords (S44). Liszt adds *cresc. stringendo* (L71), breaks the
A♭ chord downwards (L72), and ends on a full *f* chord (L73). Schumann's file has no closing dynamic
to compare.

## 4. Devices worth reusing

- **Repeat a stanza with the tune moved to the tenor** and a new figure above it (L16–27), before
  leaving the home key.
- **Tune as the top of LH chords, figure in sixths just above it.** The hands interlock, and the tune
  is voiced through the RH's lower notes (L16, L22).
- **Approach a common-tone modulation through thinning texture and silence** instead of
  reharmonising: fragments falling in register, one rolled tonic chord, a rest, then the new key with
  the common tone at the same pitch (L27–32).
- **Revoice the accompaniment chords under the tune** so the melody note isn't doubled inside them (L32–33).
- **Mark the return with colour instead of new harmony**: *pp, una corda*, new figuration (L44).
- **Tune held on top with figuration underneath in the same hand, the other hand's chord above it** (L44–46).
- **Let one tune note jump two octaves inside a phrase** (L54, L56) or echo a stepwise line at three
  octaves at once (L51).
- **Combine the two sections' textures at the climax**: the A tune over the B section's triplet
  chords (L58–64).
- **Elide one statement into the next** (L58), and **syncopate the tune against the bass** for the
  last push (L65–66).

## 5. Playing demands

- **Voicing.** L16–27: the tune is in the LH thumb, under RH sixths that sit a step or third above it.
  L44–46: RH top fingers hold the tune while the same hand plays arpeggios beneath. L49–51: the tune is
  in the LH under three-octave RH arpeggios.
- **Leaps and crossings.** L44–46: the LH goes from E♭2 to a chord around D♭6 within a half note,
  crossing above the RH. L58–64: the LH jumps from A♭1 to the tenor triplet chords on every downbeat.
  L65–66: grace-note basses, then off-beat chords. The encoding doesn't say which hand plays which staff, so all of these assume LH = staff 4.
- **Stretches.** The opening chord of L16 spans a tenth (A♭2–C4) in one staff; whether it is meant
  to be rolled is unclear.
- **Bravura.** Three-octave 16th arpeggios (L49–56), two-hand cascades (L47–48), *fff* octave chords
  over LH triplets (L58–66).
- **Ossia.** Staves 1–2 in L44–56 give a simpler version that keeps the stanza 1 layout. Presumably
  for players who can't manage the main text.
- **Difficulty.** Advanced, but less about speed than voicing and leaps. The hardest bars are L44–66
  (a judgment, not a grade).
