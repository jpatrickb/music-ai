# Planning an arrangement

Before writing any notes, write a plan. I review it, and the arrangement is written only
from an approved plan. A plan is cheap to change and cheap to judge. Planning is also where 003 went
wrong: it had a form but no style, story or harmonic picture.

Every choice in the plan cites where it comes from:
- a **reference passage**: `references/<piece>.md` and its bars
- a **theory page**: `../theory/NN-...md` and its section
- one of **my rules**, marked (mine) in `01-hymn-arrangement.md`

Say why each choice serves this song. A plan that only lists devices is the 003 failure again.

## What a plan contains

Use these headings, in this order, so plans can be compared and stored with their reviews.

1. **Brief.** Tune and source file, starting key and meter, target length, target difficulty (1–5),
   and anything the request specifies.
2. **Character and style.** Two or three sentences on the feel (e.g. a lullaby that opens out into
   radiance and returns). Then the reference passages that set the style, each with what you take
   from it: texture, figuration, register plan, a cadenza idea.
3. **Story.** For each verse, a line saying what the words are about and how it should feel. Name
   the emotional high point, and the one or two places where the text asks for a particular colour
   (text painting).
4. **Form.** A table with one row per section:
   - section, with its approximate bars
   - the verse and its words
   - key
   - **tune placement**: hand, register, and top / middle / bottom of the texture
   - texture, concretely ("RH off-beat eighth arpeggio above; tune shared between the thumbs;
     LH bass on beats 1 and 4")
   - dynamics
   - the reference passage it draws on

   Vary the tune's placement and the accompaniment between verses. Liszt keeps the tune and changes
   where it sits.
5. **Harmony.** Start from the hymnal harmony. List each departure: where, which chord(s), the
   theory page, and **why there** (which words or which moment of the form). Few departures, each
   leading somewhere.
6. **Modulations.** For each key change: the method (theory page and case), the pivot or common
   tone, how many bars it takes, how it's prepared (thinning, silence, a cadenza ending on the next
   verse's pickup), and how the new key is "dressed up" (theory/06).
7. **Melody treatment.** Where the tune is kept literally, and where it's varied (rhythm stretched
   or compressed, ornamented, octave-doubled, passed between hands). Check my rule: don't
   change melody and harmony at the same place.
8. **Motif or countermelody.** Optional: a 3–4-note idea, where it comes from (often the tune's
   own opening or cadence), and where it returns.
9. **Playing demands.** Where the difficulty is (stretches, crossings, voicing a buried tune), and
   how the left-hand rules in `01-hymn-arrangement.md` are met in each texture.
10. **Open questions for me.** Choices you're unsure of, stated as questions.

## Length

About one page. Bar numbers can be approximate. Concreteness matters more than completeness:
"tune in the thumbs, as Liebestraum No. 3 bars 1–14" is useful; "an interesting texture" is not.
