# Plan: Silent Night (draft for my review)

## 1. Brief

- **Tune:** STILLE NACHT, `../../sources/hymns/stille-nacht.krn`. B♭ major, 6/8, 12 bars, four verses.
- **Length:** about 2½–3 minutes (dotted quarter ≈ 50).
- **Difficulty target:** 4 of 5. Advanced amateur, practised.

## 2. Character and style

A lullaby that opens out into radiance and comes home to sleep. The tune never leaves; what changes
is where it sits and what surrounds it, as in Liszt's song transcriptions. Romantic-Lisztian texture
throughout, with no stylistic detours.

| Reference | What I take from it |
|---|---|
| Liebestraum No. 3, bars 1–14 (`references/liszt-liebestraum-3.md`) | Tune in the middle register, passed between the thumbs, under an off-beat arpeggio, with bass on beats 1 and 4. The 6/8 beats of Silent Night fit this figure directly. |
| Ständchen, strophe 2, bars 38–61 (`references/liszt-standchen.md`) | Tune in the tenor, with its phrase endings answered about two octaves higher (41→42, 47→48). |
| Widmung, link bars 27–31 (`references/liszt-widmung.md`) | Texture thinning and falling away into a rolled chord and silence, then the new key entering on the common tone (bar 32). |
| Un sospiro, bars 3–20 (`references/liszt-un-sospiro.md`) | Tune alternating between the hands on the beat, over a continuous arpeggio. |
| Liebestraum No. 3, cadenzas (bars 25, 59) and bars 33–37 | A cadenza from the tune's own motive that lands on the next verse's pickup; a German sixth to a cadential 6/4 to bring in a key. |
| Liebestraum No. 3, verse 3, bars 60–68 | Tune on top of the RH arpeggio, with the LH crossing above for the after-beat chords. |
| Un sospiro, bar 77 (*quasi arpa*) | Spread chords with the hand groups alternating upward, for the final chords. |

## 3. Story

- **Verse 1**, "Silent night… sleep in heavenly peace": stillness, intimacy, night.
- **Verse 2**, "Shepherds quake at the sight… heavenly hosts sing Alleluia": earth, then heaven
  answering. **Text painting:** the angels' "Alleluia" answered high above the tune.
- **Verse 3**, "Son of God, love's pure light, radiant beams…": warmth and light, a new key. **High
  point of colour.**
- **Verse 4**, "Wondrous star… with the angels let us sing, Alleluia to our King": everyone joins.
  **Climax of fullness** at "Alleluia to our King" (verse bars 7–8).
- **Coda:** back to verse 1's "sleep in heavenly peace", pp.

## 5. Harmony

Mostly the hymnal's I–V7–IV. Departures:

- **Verse 2, "Shepherds quake" (verse bars 3–4):** vi in place of the second I, so the shepherds'
  fear darkens briefly. Back to the hymnal at "glories stream" (theory/02, deceptive-cadence
  principle).
- **Verse 3, "radiant beams… redeeming grace" (verse bars 5–8):** secondary dominants that lead
  into the next chord (V7/ii → ii, V7/IV → IV), and added ninths, which "dress up" the new key
  (theory/03, theory/06).
- **Verse 4, "Alleluia to our King" (verse bars 7–8):** a bass descending by step through
  inversions under the tune, using seventh chords in third inversion that resolve to first
  inversion. I liked this in 003 (Silent Night H4-1, bars 22–25).
- **Coda:** one passing iv a few bars before the end (IV–iv–I), not in the last bar (my 003
  notes). No ♭VI anywhere.

## 6. Modulations

- **B♭ → G♭ (into verse 3).** Common-tone substitution, theory/06 Case 1. The tune's last note, B♭
  (degree 1), is held and becomes the third of G♭. It's prepared by the link's thinning and a beat of
  silence (Widmung 27–31), so there's no V7 of the new key. The new tonic gets its ninth (A♭), as
  theory/06 recommends, and verse 3 is the richest harmonically.
- **G♭ → B♭ (into verse 4).** Verse 3 ends on G♭. Adding F♭ turns it into G♭7, which is spelled as
  B♭'s German sixth (G♭–B♭–D♭–E) and resolves to F6/4, then F7, then B♭ (theory/07, treatment 1;
  Liebestraum 33–37). The cadenza unfolds the German sixth and ends on the pickup, so the
  modulation takes about two bars of free time, not a bar of dominant.

## 7. Melody treatment

- **Kept literally** in all four verses: rhythm and notes. The variety is in register and texture,
  as in Liszt.
- **Verse 2:** the echoes repeat phrase endings; the tune itself doesn't change.
- **Verse 4, "Alleluia":** the octave doubling begins here. The harmony changes there, so the
  melody notes stay exact (my rule: never change both at once).

## 8. Motif

"Heav-en-ly peace" (F–E♭–C–B♭, the last line, verse bars 11–12) is the closing motif. It opens the
piece as the intro, becomes the high echo in verse 2, and closes the coda. That frames the piece
with its own words of rest.

## 9. Playing demands

- **Verse 1:** voicing the thumbs' tune through the off-beat arpeggio; even hand changes.
- **Verse 2:** LH bass to tenor leaps, under an octave inside the figure (rule in
  `01-hymn-arrangement.md`).
- **Verse 3:** the Un sospiro alternation, the hardest part (a difficulty-4 demand). The arpeggio
  stays open in the low register (nothing closer than a fifth below about C3); any seventh sits
  high.
- **Verse 4:** LH crossings above the RH; octaves in the RH tune.
- **Coda:** spread chords; control at ppp.

## 10. Open questions for me

1. G♭ for verse 3 is a big, warm shift. Is it the colour you'd want for "love's pure light", or would
   a brighter key fit better? (Case 1 gives G♭ from B♭.)
2. Is the Un sospiro alternation right for verse 3, or does it belong in verse 4's climax instead?
3. Four verses, or would you drop one to leave room for more development (an interlude, say)?
