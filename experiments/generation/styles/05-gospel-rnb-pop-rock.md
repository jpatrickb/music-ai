# Gospel, Soul/R&B, Rock, Pop, and Neo-Soul Piano

---

## 1. Gospel Piano

**Key figures:** Thomas A. Dorsey, Roberta Martin, James Cleveland, Andraé Crouch, Richard Smallwood

### Core Concept: The Full 10-Finger Sound

Gospel piano fills every register simultaneously — no silence, no thin textures. Both hands combine
to create a dense, orchestral sound that supports congregational singing and embodies spiritual
energy.

---

### Left Hand: Bubble Bass (Arpeggiated)

The defining gospel left-hand technique is **continuous arpeggiation** — broken chord figures that
keep the bass in constant motion rather than static block notes.

**Basic bubble pattern (in C major, 4/4):**

| Beat       | Left-hand action                          |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Beat 1     | Root in bass (C, low register)            |
| "and" of 1 | 5th (G) or 3rd (E) — mid register         |
| Beat 2     | Chord tone, moving upward                 |
| "and" of 2 | Chord tone, continuing arc                |
| Beat 3     | Return to root or move to next chord tone |
| Beat 4     | Another chord tone, setting up beat 1     |

The result is a continuous eighth-note figure that arcs up through chord tones and comes back down —
a rolling, churning motion. At fast gospel tempos, this creates the "bubble" effect.

**Alternate: Walking stride left hand (older gospel, sanctified style):**

- Root on beats 1 and 3 (low register)
- Full chord on beats 2 and 4 (middle register)
- Closely related to stride but with denser chord voicings (9ths, 11ths)

---

### Right Hand: Dense Chord-Melody

- **Full 4-voice chords** in the right hand, with the melody in the top voice
- **Parallel chord motion:** Block chords moving together in the same direction, following the
  melody — similar to classical "fauxbourdon" but harmonically richer
- **Fills:** Short melodic runs between phrases, typically descending from the melody note through
  scale tones
- **Anticipations:** Chords arriving on the "and" of beat 4 (anticipating the next measure's
  downbeat)

---

### Harmonic Vocabulary

**Extended chords are standard:**

- Major 7ths and major 9ths on tonic
- Dominant 9ths and dominant 13ths on V
- Minor 11ths on ii and vi
- Half-diminished (iiø7) as substitute for the plain ii chord **The gospel turnaround sequence
  (♭VII–IV–I):**

```
B♭ major – F major – C major  (in C)
```

This plagal-inflected ending is the signature gospel cadence — the ♭VII adds a bluesy, "amen"
quality. Gospel endings often stack this with additional plagal motion: IV–I as the final
resolution.

**Circle-of-fourths vamps (the "church vamp"):** Moving through dominant 7th chords in fourth
intervals — C7 → F7 → B♭7 → E♭7 — creating tension and release over a repeated chord progression.
Congregations recognize this as the signal to repeat a chorus.

**The "gospel ending" (full cadence with added notes):**

```
V13 → IV/I (F/C) → Cmaj9
```

The final chord is always a major 9th or major 7th — resolution with color.

---

### Rhythmic Feel

**12/8 or triplet feel** is the dominant gospel feel for ballads and mid-tempo pieces:

- Eighth notes swing in triplet groups
- Melody lands on long beats; fills run through short triplet subdivisions
- The "shout" feel at climactic moments: both hands playing percussively in triplets, melody over
  churning bass **Straight 4/4 gospel (uptempo):**
- Eighth notes mostly straight (or lightly swung)
- Rhythmic emphasis on all four beats, with accents on 2 and 4
- Left hand bubble pattern continuous

## 2. Soul and R&B Piano (1950s–1980s)

**Key figures:** Ray Charles, James Booker, Dr. John, Billy Preston, Stevie Wonder, Aretha Franklin

### Core Concept: Blues Feeling Through Jazz Vocabulary

Soul piano bridges gospel's dense texture and jazz's extended harmony over a rhythm section. The
pianist no longer provides the entire harmonic foundation — a drummer and bassist handle time — so
the left hand becomes a rhythmic color instrument.

---

### Left Hand: Syncopated Bass and Chord Hits

**"And of 4" anticipation:** The defining R&B left-hand placement — the bass note or chord arrives
on the "and of 4" of the preceding bar, not on beat 1 of the new bar. This forward lean is the
primary rhythmic marker distinguishing R&B from straight gospel.

**Shell comping (low register):**

- Root or root+5th in the low register on beat 1 (or "and of 4")
- Syncopated chord hits in the mid-register on beats 2 and 3 or in off-beat positions
- No continuous bubble bass — the bassist has that role **Shuffle bass (for shuffle-feel soul):**
  Root–5th alternating pattern in eighth notes (like blues shuffle), but with a swung feel:

```
C / G / C / G  (over C7 chord, 4 eighth notes)
```

---

### Right Hand

- **Chord stabs:** Short, percussive chord hits on syncopated positions — not sustained
- **Call and response:** Right-hand melodic phrase, then a chord fill or run in response
- **Gospel fills adapted:** Descending runs, blues-inflected ornaments
- **Ray Charles innovation:** Gospel right-hand technique (block chords, fills) applied directly to
  secular R&B — the "church sound in the club"

---

### Hammond B3 Organ Influence on Piano Style

Many soul pianists think like B3 organ players:

- **Sustain everything** — the piano's sustain pedal mimics the organ's indefinite sustain
- **Register separation:** Low bass in the left hand, mid-range chords in the right, melody on top
- **Drawbar-like layering:** Dense chords that imply multiple registers simultaneously
- **Rotating speaker (Leslie) effect:** Tremolo or rapid chord repetitions simulating the Leslie
  cabinet's chorus/vibrato

---

### Harmonic Vocabulary

- **Dominant 9ths and 13ths** as primary chord color (C9, C13 everywhere)
- **Minor 7th chords** as ii substitutes or as tonic in minor key pieces
- **IV chord borrowed as IV7** (dominant quality on IV, not major 7th) — maintains blues color
- **♭VII–IV–I cadences** borrowed from gospel
- **Chromatic approach to IV:** ♯IV dim7 or ♯IV7 passing to IV — the "gospel turnaround"

## 3. Rock and Honky-Tonk Piano (1950s–1970s)

**Key figures:** Jerry Lee Lewis, Little Richard, Fats Domino, Elton John, Billy Joel, Leon Russell

### Core Concept: Pumping, Rhythmic Drive

Rock piano is percussive and physical — less about harmonic sophistication and more about rhythmic
momentum, volume, and energy.

---

### Jerry Lee Lewis: Pumping and Glissandi

**Left hand pumping:**

- Rapid octave figures in the left hand — root octave repeated in eighth or sixteenth notes
- "Pumping" creates a locomotive, driving feel
- Register: deep bass (two octaves below middle C) for maximum impact **Glissando technique:**
- Rapid slides across white or black keys
- Executed with the back of the thumbnail or index finger
- Used at phrase endings, structural moments, or for pure theatrical effect
- Ascending glissando into a downbeat: a signature dramatic gesture **Right hand:**
- Simple pentatonic or scale-based runs
- Chord riffs: short repeated melodic-harmonic figures (root–3rd–5th–♭7 of dominant chord)
- Tremolo on single notes (alternating two fingers rapidly)

---

### Little Richard: Riff-Based Intensity

**The "Tutti Frutti" / "Good Golly Miss Molly" right-hand riff pattern:**

```
♭7 – root – ♭7 – root  (repeated rhythmically over the I7 chord)
```

Short, repeating figures of 2–3 notes in the right hand, rhythmically insistent. Always over the
12-bar blues form.

**Left hand:** Rolling eighth-note bass pattern, similar to boogie-woogie but less strict:

- Root–5th alternation
- Or octave root pounding with rhythmic emphasis on beats 2 and 4

---

### Elton John: Singer-Songwriter Rock Piano

**Walking bass through chord inversions:** Bass line moves stepwise by using chord inversions to
connect root positions:

```
C major → C/B → Am → Am/G → F major → G major
```

The bass descends C–B–A–G–F–G under chord changes — smooth contrary motion between bass and melody.

**Left hand approach:**

- Root on beat 1 with chord on beats 2–4 (simple)
- Or walking bass in quarter notes through each measure
- Broken chord arpeggiation for ballads **Right hand:**
- Full chord voicings (4–5 notes) in the right hand
- Melody in the top voice of RH chord
- Rhythmically driving strummed chords for uptempo rock

---

### Billy Joel: Countermelody and Narrative

**Countermelody technique:** Right hand plays chords with an embedded inner voice that moves
independently from the melody. The inner voice creates a second melodic line beneath the top melody:

- "Piano Man" style: melody in top voice; a second, descending melodic line in the inner voices of
  the same right-hand chord
- "The Stranger" intro: independent RH line against sparse LH chords **Left hand independence:**
  Billy Joel's left hand often plays a pattern independent of the right hand's rhythm — creating
  two-voice texture even in a rock context.

---

### Harmonic Vocabulary (Rock)

- **I7–IV7–V7:** Blues-derived, all dominant 7ths (common in boogie rock and honky-tonk)
- **I–IV–V:** Plain triads, no 7ths, for driving rock anthems
- **vi–IV–I–V or I–V–vi–IV:** Common two-bar loops
- **♭VII chord:** From rock anthem vocabulary (e.g., B♭ in the key of C) — bluesy, heavy feel
- **Power chords (implied):** Piano plays the root and 5th without the 3rd — ambiguous major/minor
  quality

---

## 4. Pop and Singer-Songwriter Piano (1970s–present)

**Key figures:** James Taylor (piano-adjacent), Carole King, Billy Joel, Elton John, Tori Amos, Sara
Bareilles

### Core Concept: Supporting the Vocal

Pop piano exists primarily to support a vocal melody. The texture is transparent, the harmonic
rhythm is slow, and the piano fills the space without cluttering the vocal line.

---

### The "Money Chords" (I–V–vi–IV)

The defining pop harmonic loop:

```
C – G – Am – F  (in C major)
```

Omnipresent from the 1990s onward. The loop creates continuous forward motion without a traditional
cadence — it never fully resolves. Variations: start on vi, reverse to IV–I–V–vi, etc.

**Smooth bass motion through inversions:**

```
C major – G/B – Am – F/A
```

The bass moves C–B–A–A: chromatic/stepwise descent that makes the loop feel cohesive.

---

### Left Hand: Arpeggiated Broken Chord

The standard pop left-hand pattern:

- Root of chord in the lowest bass position
- Then arpeggiate up through the chord tones in eighth notes
- Similar to Romantic-era nocturne bass but simpler and more repetitive
- Sustain pedal held through each chord change (pedal change on each new harmony) For uptempo pop,
  the left hand may play:
- Octave root on beat 1 + chord stab on beat 3 (simplified oom-pah)
- Or quarter-note root movement

---

### Right Hand

- **Sustained chords with melody on top** — right hand holds the harmony while melody sings above
- **"Pad" voicings:** Open 5th or root–5th in the right hand, leaving space; melody fills in above
- **Sparse fills:** Short melodic runs or arpeggios between vocal phrases, never during them
- **No countermelody:** Unlike jazz or classical pop, the right hand does not compete with the vocal

---

### Voicings for Pop

| Chord   | Simple pop voicing | Extended pop voicing                         |
| ------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Cmaj    | C–E–G              | C–E–G–B (add maj7) or C–G–D (open 5th + 9th) |
| G major | G–B–D              | G–B–D–F♯ (maj7) or D–G–B–D (GBD over root)   |
| Am      | A–C–E              | A–E–G–C (rootless open)                      |
| F major | F–A–C              | F–A–C–E (maj7)                               |

## 5. Neo-Soul and Contemporary R&B (1990s–present)

**Key figures:** Robert Glasper, D'Angelo (featuring James Poyser), Erykah Badu (featuring
?uestlove), Lauryn Hill, Thundercat, Cory Henry

### Core Concept: Jazz Harmony Meets Hip-Hop Groove

Neo-soul applies jazz extended harmony and improvisation vocabulary to a hip-hop rhythmic grid. The
time feel is behind the beat, the voicings are quartal or jazz-derived, and the rhythmic placement
is deliberate.

---

### Left Hand: Sparse, Hip-Hop Influenced

**Block comping (sparse):**

- Single chord hit on beat 1 with heavy sustain, then silence
- Or chord on the "and of 2" with nothing else in the bar
- Imitates sampled chords in hip-hop production — single chord stabs rather than continuous patterns
  **Behind-the-beat pocket:** Notes placed slightly _after_ the metronomic beat — the opposite of
  bebop's forward anticipation. This creates a heavy, laid-back groove that locks with the drummer's
  snare placement.

---

### Right Hand

**Ghost notes:** Very soft chord hits between the main rhythmic placements — implied, barely audible
chords that fill space without asserting themselves. Borrowed from drummer ghost-note technique.

**Displaced chords:** Chords placed on unexpected subdivisions (sixteenth-note grid), often off the
standard eighth-note placement. The syncopation is more granular than in earlier R&B.

**Quartal voicings as default:** Instead of tertian (stacked thirds) chords, neo-soul uses stacked
fourths:

- D–G–C–F (all perfect fourths) over a D minor vamp
- C–F–B♭–E♭ as a dominant substitute Borrowed directly from McCoy Tyner and modal jazz.

---

### Robert Glasper: The Defining Synthesis

**Jazz + hip-hop methodology:**

- Left hand: hip-hop block chords or near silence
- Right hand: jazz improvisation vocabulary (bebop lines, modal runs, encircling)
- Underneath: a literal hip-hop beat from the drummer (straight 16ths on hi-hat, trap snare)
  **Specific techniques:**
- Tritone substitution in a hip-hop context: resolving D♭7 → Cmaj9 over a groove
- "Blurring" — deliberately imprecise rhythmic placement that sits between the straight grid and a
  swing feel
- Layered ostinato + improvisation: left hand holds a static quartal vamp while right hand
  improvises freely above

---

### Harmonic Vocabulary

- **Extended jazz chords:** Maj9, maj7(♯11), dom9, dom13, min11 — same as jazz but recontextualized
  over hip-hop grooves
- **Quartal harmony:** Stacked fourths as the primary voicing approach
- **Modal mixture:** Minor iv in a major-key context; ♭VII and ♭III borrowed chords
- **Chromatic mediant:** Direct third-relation moves (C major → E♭ major without preparation) for
  coloristic effect
