## Overview

Augmented sixth chords are chromatic pre-dominant chords that contain an interval of an augmented
sixth. They are primarily important in this system for their **enharmonic equivalence to dominant
seventh chords**, which enables powerful modulations by half step and major third.

**Three types:** Italian (It⁺⁶), French (Fr⁺⁶), German (Ger⁺⁶). The German Augmented Sixth is the
focus of this system because it is enharmonically identical to a dominant seventh chord — making it
the engine of modulation.

---

## Construction: General Principle

To build any augmented sixth chord:

1. Locate a sixth that can resolve to an octave
2. Adjust the interval so that **each pitch of the sixth is a half step away from the resolution
   octave**
3. Both pitches of the augmented sixth must resolve **outward by half step** to the octave **Built
   on:** The lowered (♭) sixth scale degree of the key.

**Core interval:** Augmented sixth = enharmonically a minor seventh. The difference is in the
resolution direction: a minor seventh contracts inward (both notes move inward), but an augmented
sixth **expands outward** (both notes move away from each other to the octave).

**Most crucial voice-leading rule:** The two pitches of the augmented sixth always resolve outward
by half step. This is non-negotiable.

### Construction from V–I context

Look for a V–I progression. To create an augmented sixth chord:

1. Take the fifth degree of the V chord (scale degree 2 of the key)
2. Flat it by a half step (now it is ♭2, the augmented sixth from ♭6)
3. This flattened pitch, together with ♭6 in the bass, creates the augmented sixth interval
4. **Best placement:** The flattened pitch should NOT be in the top voice (soprano)

## The Three Types

### Italian Augmented Sixth (It⁺⁶)

**Pitches (in C major):** A♭ – C – F# (♭6, 1, #4) **Interval content:** Augmented sixth (A♭–F#) +
major third above bass **Figuring:** +6 or It⁺⁶

### French Augmented Sixth (Fr⁺⁶)

**Pitches (in C major):** A♭ – C – D – F# (♭6, 1, 2, #4) **Interval content:** Augmented sixth +
major third + augmented fourth above bass **Figuring:** +4/3 or Fr⁺⁶

### German Augmented Sixth (Ger⁺⁶) ← Most Important

**Pitches (in C major):** A♭ – C – E♭ – F# (♭6, 1, ♭3, #4) **Interval content:** Augmented sixth +
major third + perfect fifth above bass **Figuring:** +6/5/3 or Ger⁺⁶

**The critical equivalence:**

```
German Aug. 6th (C major):  Ab – C – Eb – F#
Dominant 7th chord:         Ab – C – Eb – Gb  (Ab7)
```

F# and G♭ are enharmonically the same pitch. Therefore, the Ger⁺⁶ chord sounds **identical** to a
dominant seventh chord (A♭7 in C major). This dual identity is the entire basis for modulation.

## The Neapolitan Chord

The **Neapolitan chord** (N or ♭II) is a major triad built on the flatted second scale degree.

| Key     | Neapolitan chord   |
| ------- | ------------------ |
| C major | D♭ major (D♭–F–A♭) |
| G major | A♭ major (A♭–C–E♭) |
| D major | E♭ major (E♭–G–B♭) |

**Appearance:** Usually in first or second inversion (♭II⁶ is most common). **Resolution:**
Typically resolves to I in inversion (I⁶ or I⁶₄). **Finding opportunities:** Look for II chords that
resolve to I⁶₄ — substitute the Neapolitan there (same function, chromatic inflection). **Difficulty
level:** Usually the most difficult chromatic alteration to add to an existing piece. Added via
accidentals only — no new chord needed, just chromatic adjustment of an existing II chord.

## Harmonic Function

Augmented sixth chords function as **pre-dominant** chords — they lead to the cadential I⁶₄, which
leads to V7, which resolves to I.

### Standard resolution formula:

```
I  —  IV  —  I⁶₄  —  Ger⁺⁶  —  I⁶₄  —  V7  —  I
```

**Why I⁶₄, not V directly?** The Ger⁺⁶ resolves to the cadential I⁶₄ (not directly to V) because:

1. The bass note (♭6) resolves down by half step to scale degree 5 (the bass of I⁶₄)
2. The augmented sixth upper pitch (♯4) resolves up by half step to scale degree 5 in the soprano
   (an octave above the bass)
3. Both resolutions land on scale degree 5 — the bass of I⁶₄ — perfectly

---

## Voice Leading Rules

1. **The augmented sixth interval resolves outward by half step to the octave** — both pitches
   expand, each moving a half step in opposite directions to the same pitch class (scale degree 5)
2. **The ♭6 bass note** resolves DOWN by half step to scale degree 5
3. **The ♯4 upper note** resolves UP by half step to scale degree 5
4. **Do not double the augmented sixth interval** — avoid having both resolution pitches in the same
   octave range creating parallel octaves
5. The flattened pitch (♭6) works best **not in the soprano**
6. After the Ger⁺⁶ resolves to I⁶₄, proceed normally to V7 → I
7. **Adding V7–I after the new I** (when modulating) strengthens the resolution in the new key

## The Three Treatments: Using the German Aug. 6th for Modulation

The German Augmented Sixth chord's enharmonic equivalence to a dominant seventh chord enables three
distinct uses:

### Treatment 1: Standard Resolution (No Key Change)

The Ger⁺⁶ functions as itself and resolves normally within the key.

```
I  —  IV  —  I⁶₄  —  Ger⁺⁶  —  I⁶₄  —  V7  —  I
```

Same key throughout. The Ger⁺⁶ adds chromatic color and intensity to the pre-dominant area.

---

### Treatment 2: Modulate UP a Half Step

The Ger⁺⁶ is reinterpreted enharmonically as the V7 of a new key one half step higher.

```
Old key:  I  —  IV  —  I⁶₄  —  [Ger⁺⁶]  —  New I⁶₄  —  New V7  —  New I
                                    ↕ enharmonic respelling
New key:  ...                     [V7 of new key]  →  I⁶₄  →  V7  →  I
```

**How it works:**

- In C major: Ger⁺⁶ = A♭–C–E♭–F#
- Respelled as A♭7: A♭–C–E♭–G♭ (same pitches, enharmonic respelling)
- A♭7 = V7 of D♭ major → resolve to D♭ major
- **Result:** C major → D♭ major (up one half step) **Formula:** Set up old key → Ger⁺⁶ → treat as
  V7 → I⁶₄(new) → V7(new) → I(new)

---

### Treatment 3: Modulate DOWN a Half Step (The "Surprise" or "Inverted" Modulation)

A V7 chord that the listener expects to resolve normally is instead treated as a German Augmented
Sixth, causing a modulation **down** a half step.

```
Old key:  I  —  IV  —  I⁶₄  —  [V7]*  —  New I⁶₄  —  New V7  —  New I
                                  ↕ surprise reinterpretation
New key:                        [Ger⁺⁶ in new key]  →  I⁶₄  →  V7  →  I
```

**How it works:**

- In C major: V7 = G7 (G–B–D–F)
- G7 is enharmonically a German Aug. 6th in D♭ major (G = A♭♭... actually: in B major, the Ger⁺⁶
  would be G–B–D–E# = G7 enharmonically)
- More precisely: G7 functions as Ger⁺⁶ in the key of D♭ major → resolves to D♭ I⁶₄
- **Wait:** Let's be precise. To find the new key when V7 becomes Ger⁺⁶:
  - The top note of the Ger⁺⁶ (the augmented sixth itself) resolves UP to the new scale degree 5
  - In G7: F is the 7th; treated as the augmented sixth (F = E#), it resolves UP by half step to F#
    = scale degree 5 of B major
  - So G7 used as Ger⁺⁶ → I⁶₄ of B major (F# in bass) → resolves to B major
- **Result:** C major → B major (down one half step) **Practical example (from class):**

```
In C major: ...C  —  F  —  C⁶₄  —  G7*  —  B⁶₄  —  F#7  —  B major
```

G7 is the "surprise" — instead of resolving to C major (its expected target), it resolves as a Ger⁺⁶
to B major's I⁶₄.

**The "dormant" German Augmented Sixth:** Any V7 chord has the potential to be reinterpreted as a
German Aug. 6th. Every V7 is a "sleeping" Ger⁺⁶, waiting to be awakened.

## Other Treatments: Modulation to Distant Keys (Up a Major Third)

Since any dominant seventh chord can function as a German Augmented Sixth, any major triad in the
key can be given a minor seventh and treated as an Aug. 6th chord leading to a key **a major third
above**.

**The rule:** When you treat a dominant-function chord (or any chord with a minor 7th added) as a
German Augmented Sixth, you always modulate to a key a **major third higher** than the root of that
chord.

### Available "launch chords" in a major key:

| Chord used       | Add m7 to get       | Acts as Ger⁺⁶ in new key | Destination key |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------- |
| I (tonic)        | I7 (C7 in C major)  | Ger⁺⁶ of E major         | E major (up M3) |
| IV (subdominant) | IV7 (F7 in C major) | Ger⁺⁶ of A major         | A major (up M3) |
| V (dominant)     | V7 (G7 in C major)  | Ger⁺⁶ of B major         | B major (up M3) |

Modal mixture can supply additional launch chords (borrowed from parallel minor/major).

**Formula for any "other treatment" modulation:**

```
Set up old key:    I  —  IV  —  I⁶₄  —  V7  —  I
Modulate:          I  —  [any chord]7  →  (New key)I⁶₄  —  (New key)V7  —  (New key)I
```

## Worked Example: A → C# → D → B

Demonstrating all three types in sequence:

### A → C# (up a major third) — "Other Treatments" method

```
Setup A:    A  —  D  —  A⁶₄  —  E7  —  A
Modulate:   A  —  D  —  A7(as Ger⁺⁶)  —  C#⁶₄  —  G#7  —  C#
```

A7 (A–C#–E–G) = German Aug. 6th → destination = C# major (major third above A)

### C# → D (up a half step) — Treatment 2

```
Setup C#:   C#  —  F#  —  C#⁶₄  —  Ger⁺⁶  —  C#⁶₄  —  G#7  —  C#
Modulate:   C#  —  F#  —  C#⁶₄  —  Ger⁺⁶(as V7 of D)  —  D⁶₄  —  A7  —  D
```

The Ger⁺⁶ of C# major, respelled as a dominant 7th, resolves to D major.

### D → B (up a major third) — "Other Treatments" method, using IV7

```
Setup D:    D  —  G  —  D⁶₄  —  A7  —  D
Modulate:   D  —  G  —  G7(as Ger⁺⁶)  —  B⁶₄  —  F#7  —  B
```

G7 (G–B–D–F) = German Aug. 6th of B major (major third above G)

## Inversions of Augmented Sixth Chords

### The "Dormant" or "Surprise" Principle (Inverted Treatment)

In the traditional (non-inverted) use, you play an obvious Ger⁺⁶ chord, then modulate **up** a half
step (Treatment 2).

In the inverted/surprise use, you first establish a V7 chord normally, then reveal it was "secretly"
a Ger⁺⁶ and resolve it as one — modulating **down** a half step (Treatment 3).

**Practical exercise (Three Treatments exercise):** Play all three treatments in a single
transposition sequence without stopping, cycling through all keys:

```
[In current key:]
Treatment 1:  I–IV–I⁶₄–Ger⁺⁶–I⁶₄–V7–I
Treatment 2:  I–IV–I⁶₄–Ger⁺⁶(as new V7)–New I⁶₄–New V7–New I  (now in key a half step up)
Treatment 3:  [In new key:] I–IV–I⁶₄–V7*(surprise Ger⁺⁶)–Old I⁶₄–Old V7–Old I  (back to original key)
```
